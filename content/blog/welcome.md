For a while now, this site has been a shelf of products: a security app for the Mac, a video generator, an offline messenger, a handful of games. Each one has its own page that says what it does and what it keeps. What the site never had was a place to talk about everything *around* those products — the technology they are built on, the tools we try and throw away, and the threats we keep running into while making software that is meant to keep people safe.

This blog is that place.

## What you will find here

Posts fall into four kinds, and each one is labelled so you can read only what you came for:

- **Technology** — how things actually work, written for people who use technology rather than people who sell it. AI models on your own device, what a VPN does and does not do, why an app asks for the permissions it asks for.
- **Reviews** — hardware, apps and services we have used ourselves, for long enough to have an opinion. What was good, what was not, and who it is for.
- **Security alerts** — scams, malware campaigns and vulnerabilities that are hitting real people right now, with the concrete steps to check whether you are affected and what to do about it.
- **News** — releases and changes to our own products, when there is something worth saying beyond a changelog.

## The rules every post follows

The products on this site are held to one line: no trackers, no accounts, nothing collected that does not need to be. The writing here gets rules of its own.

1. **Plain language first.** If a term needs explaining, it gets explained. If it does not need to be there, it goes.
2. **Sources, always.** Claims about a threat, a product or a study link to where they came from, so you can check them yourself.
3. **Honest reviews.** If we review something we did not pay for, or something that competes with one of our own products, the post says so at the top.
4. **Useful alerts, not scary ones.** A security alert is only worth publishing if it tells you what to do. Each one ends with steps, not with fear.
5. **Corrections in the open.** When a fact changes or we get something wrong, the post is updated and says what changed and when.

> [!TIP] How to follow along
> New posts show up on the [blog page](/blog) and on the home page. If you use a feed reader, subscribe to the [RSS feed](/blog/feed.xml) — no account, no email address, nothing to sign up for.

## Tell us what to write about

Some of the best posts start as a question. If there is a tool you want reviewed, a scam that just reached your inbox, or a piece of technology you would like explained without the jargon, write to [contact@aibachkhoa.com](mailto:contact@aibachkhoa.com). If you have found a security problem in one of our own products, the same address reaches us — please send it there before posting it anywhere else, so we can fix it first.
