GenVideo: Master Tool — for Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS

Last updated: 6 September 2026

GenVideo makes videos on a server, so unlike a purely local tool it genuinely does send your material somewhere. This page says exactly what leaves your device, what is kept, for how long, and who else sees it. Where the answer is "nothing", it says so plainly; where something is sent, it is listed rather than summarised away.

Summary

GenVideo: Master Tool is published by AIBachKhoa (Son Tran), Vietnam. It turns a topic or a script into a narrated video, and the generation runs on our server rather than on your machine.

There are no user accounts. We do not ask for your name, your email or a password, and we do not have them. What identifies a paid licence is a random 64-character code your device generates on first run — it is not derived from your hardware and it says nothing about you.

The text you type is sent, because that is the job. A video cannot be written from a topic that stays on your machine. What is sent, and what happens to it afterwards, is set out below.

Why there is no account

Asking someone to create an account before they can buy is asking them to hand over an email address that the product does not need. So a licence belongs to the machine instead: the app generates a random code, keeps it in the operating system's keychain — Keychain on Apple platforms, the encrypted keystore on Android, the credential store on Windows — and that code is the only thing the server knows about the buyer.

This has a real cost, and hiding it would be dishonest: a licence lives on one machine. Buying on a laptop does not unlock a phone. The Plans screen shows the code so you can copy it to another machine yourself, and clearing the app's data or reinstalling can lose it — which is why the app tells you to keep it, and why support cannot find a payment without it.

What the app sends

Only these things leave your device, and only when the action that needs them happens:

What When, and why
Your topic or script, and the render settings When you launch a render. This is the material the video is made from — the narration is written from it, the image prompts are drawn from it, and the voice reads it.
Your licence code With every request that checks or spends your quota, and when you buy a plan. It is how the server knows which licence is being used.
A music file you upload Only if you upload one. It joins the library on the server so later renders can use it.
A reference audio path or URL Only if you fill that field in, and only for voice-cloning workflows that support it.
Your own API key, on Bring your own key Only on that plan, and only so the render can be billed to your account with the model provider instead of ours.

What it never sends

  • No name, email address, phone number or postal address — the app has no field to type them into
  • No advertising identifier, and no advertising or analytics SDK is embedded at all
  • No contacts, calendar, location or photo library
  • No hardware identifier: the licence code is random, not derived from your device
  • No crash or usage telemetry, and no behavioural tracking of any kind

What stays on your device

Kept locally, and never uploaded:

  • Your licence code, in the operating system's keychain
  • The draft you were last working on, so it survives closing the app
  • Interface preferences: language, light or dark theme
  • How many free trial videos this device has used
  • Any video you chose to download

Uninstalling the app, or clearing its data, removes all of it — including the licence code, which is why it is worth copying somewhere first.

Who else processes it

Making a video means handing parts of the work to other services. These are the only ones, and each sees only what its step needs:

Service What it receives
OpenAI Your topic or script, to write the narration and the image prompts, and to generate the artwork. On Bring your own key this runs on your account, not ours.
Microsoft Edge text-to-speech The narration lines, to read them aloud.
Supabase Your licence code, plan, expiry and quota counter. No content and nothing personal.
PayPal · Google Play The purchase itself. We never see your card or bank details; they are handled entirely by the store you buy through.

Nothing is sold, rented or handed to advertisers or data brokers. Disclosure to anyone else happens only where the law requires it.

Payment

Desktop builds are paid through PayPal; the Android build is paid through Google Play, because Google requires it for digital goods used inside an app. In both cases your payment details go to them and never to us — we receive a confirmation that a payment happened, attached to your licence code.

Subscriptions renew monthly until cancelled, and can be cancelled at any time from PayPal or from Google Play. The link is on the Plans screen inside the app. Cancelling stops the next renewal and leaves the current period intact — you keep what you already paid for.

How long things are kept

  • Rendered videos and their source text: kept on the server so the app can fetch them, and removed on request. Write in with your licence code and they go.
  • Licence records: kept while the licence exists, and afterwards only as long as tax and accounting rules require.
  • Payment event log: kept so a "I paid and it did not unlock" can be answered — without it there is no way to check.
  • Music you uploaded: kept until you delete it from the Music screen.

Permissions the app asks for

Permission Why
INTERNET To reach the render server. Without it the app cannot make anything.
WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE Only on Android 9 and older, and only to save a finished video to your gallery. On Android 10 and later no permission is needed for that at all.

There is no camera, microphone, location or contacts permission, because there is nothing in the app that would use one.

Children

GenVideo is not aimed at children under 13 and collects nothing that identifies a person, so it holds no children's data. It is a production tool for people who publish video.

Your rights

Because there is no account, most of what you would normally have to ask for you can simply do:

  • Erase local data: uninstall the app, or clear its data
  • Delete your videos and their source text: write in with your licence code
  • Ask what is held against a licence code: write in and we will tell you
  • Stop paying: cancel from PayPal or Google Play, no request to us needed

One consequence worth stating: since we hold no name or email, a request has to come with the licence code. It is the only thing that connects a person to a record, and without it we genuinely cannot tell which record is yours.

Security

Traffic runs over HTTPS. The licence table denies every read and write from the app's own key — only the server-side functions can touch it, so the key shipped inside every copy of the app can read nothing, not even its own row.

Payment confirmations are verified against PayPal's signature or Google's servers before a licence is granted. The app's claim that a payment happened is never enough on its own; if it were, anyone could send that claim.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. A change that affects what is collected or who receives it will be announced in the app before it takes effect, not slipped in quietly.
