Dungeon of the Fallen Blade

Android · pixel action roguelite

A hundred depths. One blade.

A hand-made pixel-art action roguelite. Cut your way down a hundred procedurally generated depths, take a boon at every gate, and meet the lord waiting on every fifth. Every run rebuilds the dungeon from scratch — nothing is memorised, everything is earned.

The descent

Twenty acts of five, and a lord at the bottom of each.

An act sets the colour of its stone, how often a pit is fire rather than spikes, which music plays and which enemies patrol it. Its first four depths are ordinary warrens; its fifth is an arena holding that act's lord. So a boss waits on depth 5, 10, 15 … 100.

Combat

Swings with weight.

Three ways to answer a room, and a stamina bar that makes you choose between them.

A three-hit sword combo

Chain the swings and the third lands as a heavy finisher, with more reach and more knockback than the two before it. Break the chain and it starts again.

A bow for the fights you would rather not walk into

Arrows cost stamina, so a room cleared at range is a room you enter with nothing left for a roll. The Deep Quiver boon makes the trade worth more.

A dodge roll with real invulnerability

Not a dash with a hopeful hitbox — the roll grants actual i-frames, and costs stamina to use. Rolling through a lord's charge is the game's main defensive move.

Movement that reads the room

Hold jump to rise higher, tap again to double jump, and press down with jump to drop through a wooden platform. Every ledge in the dungeon is proved reachable with these alone.

The dungeon

A floor is not a corridor — it runs in four directions.

Every depth is built fresh, with rooms joined left, right, up and down — so a floor is ground to move around in, not a line to walk.

Rooms wired in all four directions

Rooms sit side by side and stacked on top of one another, so a depth climbs as much as it spreads. Side doors join neighbours on a row; shafts with drop-through walkways and staggered ledges join the floors above and below.

Loops and dead ends, not one route

Extra links open between rooms so a floor is never a single corridor. The deeper you go, the more a depth opens out into ground you can move around in rather than a route you follow.

A key, a gate, and an exit that can be anywhere

The entrance is a random dead end and the exit is the dead end furthest from it — above, below, left or right of where you start. From depth 2 the portal is sealed behind a barred gate whose key waits in another dead end, guarded.

Pits kill, and every one of them can be cleared

Falling into spikes or fire is death on the spot, for you and for anything chasing you. No pit is ever wider than a single jump clears, and no route ever asks you to cross a deadly tile.

Every floor is guaranteed completable

No depth reaches you without being checked end to end: the key reachable with the gate shut, the portal reachable with it open, and no room sealed off. A layout that does not hold up is discarded and built again.

And every depth is checked for pacing

All hundred depths are reviewed for how a clean run plays out. A floor that would drag, one that could kill too quickly, or one that crowds a room too hard is rebuilt before it ever reaches you.

The lords

A lord is not a big orc with more health.

Each carries a short list of moves with their own cooldowns and range bands, and takes the first one that is off cooldown and in range. They break into a second phase at half health; most break again at a third, coming back faster, throwing longer volleys and calling deeper reinforcements.

Move What it does
LeapCrouches, then jumps onto where you are standing and lands in a shockwave. This is the answer to a ledge: a lord that cannot walk to you arrives anyway.
ChargeWinds up, then dashes the length of the arena with its hitbox live. Running it into a wall staggers it — the miss is your window.
QuakeThe longest tell in the game, because it is the widest hit in the game: it clears five body-widths of ground on both sides.
FanA wall of bolts across an arc. Sidestepping alone will not clear it.
MortarLobbed bolts that arc over cover and come down where you are standing.
ContactStanding inside the biggest lords costs health on its own.

A fight you cannot wait out

A lord's moves are spaced out at the start rather than thrown at you all at once, and the gaps between them tighten the longer the fight runs. Waiting it out on a ledge only brings the leap sooner.

Size is derived from health

How big a thing looks is how much punishment it will take, and that reading holds for every enemy in the game. Each of the twenty lords has its own size, colour, health, reach, volley and roar.

Four arena layouts

Arenas come in four ledge patterns of increasing length, so twenty fights do not all happen in the same hall repainted. Every ledge sits within reach of a double jump.

Boons

One choice at every gate.

Level up as you kill, then take a boon on the way down. Three are offered, one is yours — and the deeper you go, the more it takes.

Death ends the run. There is no checkpoint and nothing respawns where you fell — the results screen's only way back into the dungeon resets you to depth one with the levels, boons and gold gone.

Built for a phone

Touch controls that hold a direction and swing at once.

A floating analogue stick on the left half of the screen and a compact action cluster in the bottom-right corner: attack, jump, bow, dodge, potion, plus a use pad that only appears next to a chest or a gate.

  • Every pad is multi-touch. Hold a direction, attack and jump at the same moment. The touch targets are about 30% larger than the art so they stay easy to hit.
  • A tall screen gets a wider view, not black bars. The view keeps its height and widens to fill the device, and the HUD re-anchors itself on rotation.
  • Whole pixels on real hardware. On common phone resolutions the art scales by a whole number, so nothing is softened by resampling.
  • On automatically, and switchable. The pads turn on for Android, iOS or anything reporting a touchscreen; Options → Touch pad cycles auto / on / off. Gamepads and keyboards work throughout.

No strings

Nothing to buy, nothing behind a paywall.

The game itself runs on the device. Settings and your best depth live in the app's own save file; the only thing that ever leaves the phone is a leaderboard score, and only once you sign in to Google Play Games.

  • No ads. None anywhere in the game, and no ad network in the build.
  • No in-app purchases. The whole hundred depths ship in the download.
  • Signing in is optional. Every depth, boon and lord is playable without an account — Play Games only adds the leaderboards and the achievements.
  • Plays with the connection off. The app asks for the internet permission so it can reach Play Games; drop the connection and the game plays exactly the same, minus posting a score.

Read the privacy policy

Which Android versions does it run on?

Android 7.0 and up, on arm64-v8a and armeabi-v7a. Google Play splits the bundle per device, so a phone downloads roughly a third of the whole build.

Does it need an internet connection?

Not to play. The app declares the internet permission because it talks to Google Play Games for the leaderboards and achievements, but every depth, boon and lord works with the connection off — you simply do not post a score while it is.

What is sent to Google Play Games?

Three leaderboard scores — deepest descent, richest run and fastest descent — and progress on eight achievements, tied to your own Play Games profile and handled by Google under its own privacy policy. None of it happens unless you sign in, and nothing else leaves the phone.

Is there a save between runs?

Only your settings and the deepest depth you have reached. A run itself has no checkpoint: death resets you to depth one with the levels, boons and gold gone. That is the roguelite part. Signed in, your best depth, richest run and fastest descent also go to the leaderboards.

Can I play it with a gamepad, or on a desktop?

Yes. Keyboard and gamepad bindings are first-class, and the on-screen pads can be forced off under Options → Touch pad. The art scales by whole pixels, so it stays crisp on a handheld or a desktop.

How violent is it?

Fantasy violence with pixel-art blood effects, and nothing else sensitive. That lands around ESRB Teen / PEGI 12 / IARC 12+, and 13+ is the natural audience.
