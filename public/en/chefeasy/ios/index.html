ChefEasy

iPhone & iPad · home cooking

One step at a time, not one wall of text.

The hard part of a recipe is not the ingredient list. It is the moment your hands are covered in flour and you need to know what comes next. ChefEasy walks you through one task at a time, each with its own timer, and keeps the screen awake while it does.

Cooking mode

One step per screen, and a clock for it.

A printed recipe makes you find your place again every time you look down. Cooking mode does the opposite: it shows only the task in front of you, counts down that task alone, and moves on when you say so.

Exactly one thing on screen

The current step fills the view; the rest collapse into a progress list underneath. No hunting for the line you were on in a dense block of text.

Timers on tasks, not on the dish

"Soak the mung beans 6–8 hours" and "chop the spring onion" are not the same kind of time. Each task carries its own clock, starts when its turn comes, and has a done-early button for when your stove is faster than the recipe.

The screen stays awake

While cooking mode is open the app keeps the display on. You never have to touch the phone with oily hands just to read step three.

Ingredients you can tick off

The ingredient list has checkboxes and a counter in the corner. Tick them at the market or as you prep — what is left is always obvious.

Voice

When your hands are busy, say it.

A floating microphone button sits on the home screen, the recipe page and inside cooking mode. Say a dish to find it, or move between steps without touching the device.

  • Search by speaking. Say a dish name and the app filters all 44 recipes, matching both the original name and its translation.
  • Command while cooking. Next step, back, start or stop a timer — by voice, with both hands full.
  • Only on when you press it. The microphone does not listen in the background. It opens after you tap the button and closes when you stop speaking.

Worth stating plainly: turning speech into text is done by the operating system's own recognition service, so that audio goes to Google's servers on Android or Apple's on iPhone and iPad. ChefEasy never receives the recording, never stores it, and has no server to store it on. If you would rather not, simply never press the microphone button — nothing else in the app needs it.

Permissions

Two permissions, and only when you press.

iOS asks you before granting either, and both are requested at the moment you tap the microphone — not when the app opens.

  • Microphone. To hear what you say when searching for a dish or giving a command while cooking.
  • Speech recognition. To turn that into text. Apple's service does this work, so the audio goes to Apple's servers.
  • Nothing beyond those two. No location, no contacts, no camera, no photo library, no push notifications.

Languages

Five languages, recipes included.

Not just buttons and menu labels. Dish names, ingredients and every cooking step carry their own translation — and anything not yet translated falls back to English rather than showing blank.

Offline

The recipes live on your device.

All 44 recipes and their photographs ship inside the app and unpack on first launch. From the second launch onward it opens straight away — including in the corner of the kitchen where the signal dies.

  • Recipes read with no connection. Turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data and the cooking side of the app is unchanged.
  • No account. No sign-up, no login, no user profile. Open the app and use it.
  • Favourites stay on the device. Your favourites and viewing history live in the app's own storage and are not sent anywhere.
  • The network is used for two things. Loading ads on the free tier, and handling payment when you subscribe to Pro.

Read the privacy policy

Which iPhones and iPads does it run on?

iOS 15.0 or later, on both iPhone and iPad.

Are there ads?

The free tier shows an ad when the app opens. Nothing interrupts you mid-recipe. A Pro subscription removes them entirely.

Does it need an internet connection?

Not to read or cook a recipe. A connection is used for ads, for App Store payment, and for voice search.

Does the app collect my data?

We run no servers and receive no data about you. Favourites, history and settings live in the app's own storage on your device. The third parties are Google, for ads, and Apple, for payment and speech recognition.

What is the microphone for?

Only for finding a dish and for commands while cooking, and only after you press the microphone button. The app does not listen in the background, does not record, and keeps no audio.

Is it suitable for children?

The content is cooking recipes, with nothing sensitive in it. But the app carries ads and in-app purchases, so a shared device is worth a store purchase PIN.

ChefEasy Pro

Optional, and spelled out.

The free tier gives you every Vietnamese dish in full — no open limit, no locked steps. Pro is a subscription that removes the ads and unlocks the other cuisines.

The question The answer
What Pro unlocksRemoves every ad, unlocks the Chinese, Japanese and Korean dishes, and adds new recipes each week.
What you lose without itNothing on the Vietnamese side. Every Vietnamese dish, the guided cooking mode, the timers, voice search and offline use are all free.
What it costsA monthly or a yearly plan, auto-renewing, billed by the App Store. The price appears in your own currency inside the app.
How to cancelIn Settings → your name → Subscriptions, any time. There is no retention flow in the app to argue with you, and we never see your card.
If you cancelAds return and the Chinese, Japanese and Korean dishes lock again at the end of the period you paid for. The Vietnamese dishes stay.
