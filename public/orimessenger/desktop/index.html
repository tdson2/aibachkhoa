Chat that keeps talking when the Internet stops.

OriMessenger sends your messages over Bluetooth or the same local network when the other person is close, and falls back to the Internet only when they are not. Every message is end-to-end encrypted — there is no account and no phone number.

Why OriMessenger

Three ways to reach someone, in this order.

Most messengers assume the Internet is always there. OriMessenger does not. When the person you are talking to is next to you, the app chooses the shortest path automatically.

1. Bluetooth — when you are within a few metres

The app advertises itself over Bluetooth Low Energy and discovers other Ori users nearby. When you are within roughly 10 to 30 metres, messages hop directly, phone to phone, without touching a router or a cell tower. No Internet needed at all.

2. Wi-Fi / LAN — same network

On the same Wi-Fi or wired network, devices find each other automatically and exchange messages inside the local network. This works on captive-portal Wi-Fi where outbound Internet is blocked, and it is fast enough for photos.

3. Internet — when neither of the above works

Only when Bluetooth and LAN both fail does the message go through a relay. The relay is a dumb pipe: names, profiles, message bodies and attachments are all encrypted end to end, so it cannot read any of them. It does see who sent to whom and when — it needs that to route the message — and holds it for at most 30 days.

How it works

End-to-end encryption, spelled out.

This is not marketing. Here is exactly what is signed, encrypted, and stored where.

01

Keys stay on the device

Each install generates its own pair of identity keys. The private half never leaves the device. Your ID is derived from the public half.

02

Message payloads are sealed

Message bodies, sender/recipient names, avatars and attachments are encrypted with a per-conversation key negotiated between the two devices. The relay only ever sees the ciphertext plus the delivery details it needs to route it: sender ID, recipient ID and a timestamp.

03

Friend requests carry no profile

A request contains only the sender's ID and public key. Your display name and status are only sent, encrypted, after the other side accepts.

04

History stays on the device

Chat history is kept in a local database inside your user profile. Uninstalling the app or deleting the app data removes it. There is no cloud backup that we control.

Built for places the Internet is unreliable.

A concert, a metro tunnel, a campsite, a captive-portal hotel Wi-Fi, a country where messenger apps get blocked at the DNS layer. OriMessenger keeps working because it does not depend on a single network being up.

  • No phone number

    Sign-up is a 16-character ID generated on your device. There is no SMS gateway to leak your number to.

  • No account

    No email, no password, no recovery flow. Losing the device is losing the identity, and that is by design.

  • Uses the Play billing sandbox: no

    The app is free. There are no subscriptions, no in-app purchases and no advertising SDK.

  • Open protocol, closed to strangers

    You can only be messaged by someone who already has your ID. The nearby list is opt-in from the same screen.
