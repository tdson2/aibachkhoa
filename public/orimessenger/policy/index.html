Privacy Policy

OriMessenger — for Windows, Linux and Android.

Last updated: 25 September 2026

Short version. OriMessenger has no account and no phone number. Every message body, name, avatar and attachment is encrypted end to end between the two devices. Where the app has to reach the Internet, the server it talks to sees only a recipient ID and a bag of ciphertext, and nothing about who you are.

Who publishes this app

OriMessenger is published by AIBachKhoa (Son Tran), based in Hanoi, Vietnam. Contact details are at the bottom of the page.

What is stored on the device

On the first launch the app generates an X25519 identity key pair and derives your 16-character user ID from the public half. All of this stays on your device. The following is written to the app's own profile directory (on Windows: %APPDATA%/OriMessenger; on Linux: ~/.config/OriMessenger; on Android: the app's private storage):

  • Your identity key pair. The private half never leaves the device.
  • Your chosen display name, status text, avatar colour and background.
  • Your friend list, blocked list, and any pending friend requests.
  • The chat history, in a local SQLite database. Attachments are stored as files next to it.
  • Per-conversation session keys negotiated with your contacts.
  • App preferences: language, light/dark mode, sending channel default.

Uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes all of the above. There is no cloud backup that we control.

The three network paths a message can take

OriMessenger tries paths in this order and stops at the first one that works:

PathWhat travels, and to where
Bluetooth (BLE) The device advertises a short service identifier over Bluetooth Low Energy and scans for other devices doing the same. Messages hop directly, phone to phone. Nothing leaves your immediate area — there is no cell tower or router involved.
Wi-Fi / LAN Devices on the same local network find each other with mDNS (_ori._tcp.local.) and connect directly by IP. Traffic stays inside the local network. This works even when outbound Internet is blocked.
Internet relay Only when the two above have failed. The device opens a TLS connection to the relay operated by AIBachKhoa and hands off an envelope addressed by recipient ID. The next section describes exactly what the relay sees.

What the relay server sees

The Internet relay is a queue. Each device connects to it with TLS. When your device has a message for someone whose ID it knows, it hands the relay:

  • The recipient's user ID (16 characters, derived from their public key).
  • The ciphertext of the message, sealed with a per-conversation key that only your device and the recipient's have. This includes any attachment bytes and any human-readable metadata such as your name, avatar or status.
  • The network-layer information that any TLS connection carries: your IP address and a temporary connection ID.

The relay does not see:

  • The message text or media contents. It has no key to decrypt them.
  • Your display name, avatar, status, contact list, or which conversation the message belongs to.
  • Any phone number, email address, or third-party account. There is none associated with your ID.

Envelopes are held only until the recipient device fetches them, or up to 30 days, whichever comes first. After that they are deleted from the queue.

Sharing with third parties

We do not sell user data, and we do not share it with advertising networks. The only situations in which any information related to you leaves our systems are:

  • Infrastructure providers that host the relay (a Vietnamese cloud provider) — they see the encrypted envelopes flowing through the queue, no more than the relay itself does.
  • Law enforcement, when we are legally required to respond to a valid request. Given that the relay only holds ciphertext and delivery metadata, the useful surface is small; we will say so if asked.
  • A successor entity, in the event of a merger or acquisition. Any transfer inherits this policy.

Retention and deletion

Chat history, key material and profile settings live on your device until you delete them. Uninstalling the app removes them, on every platform.

Undelivered envelopes on the relay are dropped once the recipient fetches them, or after 30 days. Delivered envelopes are dropped immediately.

Server-side logs of relay traffic (TLS connection metadata, timestamps, byte counts — no message payloads and no user IDs) are retained for up to 14 days for operational debugging, then rotated out.

Children's privacy

OriMessenger is not directed at users under 13. In jurisdictions that require parental consent, we follow the local rule. If you believe a child has created an ID and you want it removed, write to the address below with the ID; we can delete the corresponding relay queue.

Your rights

Because there is no account, most rights you would normally exercise against a company are yours to exercise directly on the device: access, correction, deletion and export all mean opening the app or the profile directory. If you need something we can help with — for example deleting your relay queue by ID — write to the address below.

Security

All Internet traffic uses TLS 1.3 to the relay. End-to-end encryption uses a curve25519 handshake and an authenticated stream cipher (ChaCha20-Poly1305) for the payload. The Windows installer, the Android APK and the Play AAB are signed with a certificate whose fingerprint is:

No system is fully secure and we do not promise otherwise. If you find a vulnerability, please email contact@aibachkhoa.com with the details rather than posting it publicly.
