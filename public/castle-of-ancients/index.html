CASTLE OF ANCIENTS

iPhone · iPad · pixel tower defense

One road. One castle. Hold it.

Drag a guard post from the side bar, drop it on the grass, and watch a stretch of open road turn into a killing field. Kill for gold, spend the gold on deeper defences, and hold the line to the last wave.

The loop

Build, kill, build again.

Everything in the game runs off one number. Gold comes from things you killed, and it is the only thing that buys the defence that kills the next lot. Spend it in the wrong place and the road tells you so about forty seconds later.

Drag it where you want it

One finger takes a card off the side bar and drops it on the tile you chose. Or tap the card, then tap the tile — the same build, two ways round, so it works whichever hand you have free.

Gold is the whole economy

There is no second currency, no energy bar and nothing to top up. Every coin came off something that died on your road, and the only question the game ever asks is where the next one goes.

Upgrades put a body on the board

Three levels per post, and each one is visible: another archer on the roof, another warrior standing in the road. You can see what you paid for without opening a stats panel.

A wrong post is not a lost run

Sell any post back for 60% of everything you poured into it and rebuild somewhere that works. The map is allowed to teach you something halfway through a wave.

Guard posts

Four posts, four different answers.

None of the four is the best one. Each closes a door the others leave open, and every map is really a question about which door matters here.

Archer Tower

70 gold

Fast single-target fire, and the only cheap thing you own that can touch a flier. The backbone of every line you will ever build.

Bolt Yard

110 gold

Explosive bolts that splash across a packed column, and armour only stops half of it. Completely blind to anything airborne.

Barracks

90 gold

Barely shoots. It sends three warriors out to stand in the road and BLOCK it, holding the enemy still while everything else does the killing.

Monastery

130 gold

Magic that ignores armour completely and slows what it hits. The answer to skeletons, and the answer to the minotaur walking behind them.

Armour is subtraction, not a percentage

Every point of armour comes straight off the damage of the hit that lands. That is why one post is never enough, and why the counter to a heavy column is a different post rather than a bigger one.

  • Arrows take armour full in the face. A skeleton with 5 armour eats most of an archer volley and keeps walking.
  • Explosions get through half of it. Bolt Yards stay useful against armour longer than arrows do — right up until something flies.
  • Magic ignores it entirely. The Monastery does its full number to the toughest thing on the map, and slows it on the way through.
  • Nothing is ever fully immune. Even the weakest hit against the heaviest armour still carries 20% through, so no wave is ever unwinnable — only expensive.

Maps

Twelve maps, two ways to play.

Story teaches, campaign lets you set the terms. Both are the same game; only the guard rails differ.

Story — five missions, each one an idea

They unlock one after another, and each map is built around a single lesson rather than a difficulty number.

  • Gatemeadow. One straight road and open grass on both sides. Nothing to learn but the loop itself.
  • Broken Gorge. The road doubles back on itself, and every bend is a place one tower covers twice.
  • Shallow Ford. Water squeezes the building space, and the first fliers arrive to ignore your blockers.
  • Muster Crossroads. Two columns march in and merge halfway. One line, built where they meet.
  • Siege of the Old Keep. A 79-tile spiral with the keep ringed in the middle and two bosses inside the ring.

Campaign — seven larger maps, unlocked from the start
Bigger than the screen, so you pinch to zoom and drag to pan. From a windswept S-bend to a three-gate river delta, a 98-tile spiral, a mountaintop keep with a single ramp, and a castle beset on four sides at once.

Your rules

Four dials, and every one comes off.

Set before each campaign run. Each dial has a setting that removes the limit outright — so the same seven maps are a tight economy puzzle or a sandbox, and that is your call rather than a difficulty menu's.

Touch

Built for a hand, not shrunk to fit one.

The touch layout is not the desktop layout with bigger buttons. It is a different set of numbers, chosen because a finger is not a cursor.

  • The cards are taller and the side bar is wider, because a thumb reaching across a landscape screen is less accurate than a mouse that never misses.
  • The drag threshold is wider, so a tap that wobbles two pixels is still a tap and not a half-started build you have to cancel.
  • The tower ghost lifts clear of your fingertip, because your hand covers exactly the tile you are aiming at — the one place a preview must not be.
  • Pinch to zoom, one finger to pan on the campaign maps, which are wider than any screen you own.
